Conclusions and solutions

The real fix: stop recovering wires and poles through per-kilowatt-hour rates

Fix the rate structure

  1. Move more grid cost into fixed charges. The base services charge ($24.15 / $12 FERA / $6 CARE) is the first step. Once the fixed pot no longer rides on kWh, self-generation stops shifting costs to neighbors.
  2. Income-graduated fixed charge. Spreads grid upkeep in proportion to income, building on that structure, so lower-income households aren't squeezed.
  3. Move some grid costs off rates entirely. Fund part of the wires and poles from the state budget. Precedent: Prop 4 (2024) and SB 254 (2025).
  4. Charge by peak demand or connection size. Reflects what the grid is built to carry, not energy used.

These apply far more to the ~9,800 MW rooftop fleet and its $3.8B cost shift than to balcony kits.

Structural considerations

Small residential storage

Batteries sized to bridge sunset to bedtime, easing the evening peak that rooftop solar worsens.

A SOMAH-style incentive

Government funds and distributes kits far more efficiently than a utility program. Caveat: SOMAH doesn't cover plug-in kits today (licensed contractor, interconnection, deed-restricted housing), so it needs a program change.
